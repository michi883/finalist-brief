# A/B 1: Gemini-first vs Claude baseline on Pulse (2026-09-26)

**Question.** Can Gemini-first keep the Claude baseline's deep-review quality at a much lower cost?

**Answer.** Not with Gemini 3.8 Flash at medium thinking. It was 4.7× cheaper, and every claim it kept is accurate. But it missed the findings a judge most needs, and the loss is in the `generate` stage.

## Setup

- **Submission.** `pulse-the-autonomous-corporate-memory-knowledge-graph`, unseen. The repository snapshot is `6e03350860e6`. The demo is 50.5 s: 17 frames, no captions on YouTube.
- **Frozen pipeline.** No prompt or rule was changed while looking at Pulse. Both arms shared `acquire` and `repo_facts` and ran from `extract` with `--no-publish`, in parallel.
- **Harness changes before the run** (not pipeline logic):
  - Gemini route options are now part of the response cache key.
  - The ledger records call duration and retries.
  - `run.py` gained `--from` and per-stage timing.

| Arm | observe | generate + repairs | verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| A `claude-baseline` | Claude Opus 5.5 | Claude Opus 5.5 | Claude Opus 5.5 |
| B `gemini-first` | Gemini 3.8 Flash, medium thinking | Gemini 3.8 Flash, medium thinking | Claude Opus 5.5 |

## Blind quality review

One reader saw only the two reviews, relabelled X and Y at random, plus Social Fabric as the clarity reference. It had copies of the writeup lines, the repository snapshot and the frames, and spot-checked 8 references in X and 9 in Y. The labels were sealed until the verdict: **X = Claude, Y = Gemini-first.** The reader was a Claude model instance, which could carry a family bias. Its decisive points, however, are checkable omissions, not style.

| Criterion | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Summary | **Claude, clearly.** "replies are assembled from stored text without an AI model", against Gemini's "ask an AI assistant questions", which repeats the team's framing. |
| 2. Idea graph | Claude, slightly. Both are fair. Gemini's "Bento grid" dashboard is a UI detail rather than a concept. |
| 3. Integration graph | **Claude, clearly.** Claude's details carry the findings ("Raw events only", "3 simulated emails", "No AI model", "Needs 'decision'", "Alerts hard-coded"), and its edge "palette only" shows the real wiring. Gemini has a generic "Serverpod endpoints" node and no conflict path. |
| 4. Observations | Claude, slightly. Gemini's "Enron 500k corpus → 3 simulated records" was the best single observation in either review. |
| 5. Judge questions | **Claude, strongly.** Claude asks three findings-backed questions: was the demo recorded from this repository (its browser alerts and chat text are not in the code)? Why does conflict detection look for `'decision'` when the importers write `'email_decision'`/`'slack_decision'`? What turns GitHub events into graph nodes, when `processKnowledgeGraph` is never scheduled and the `.json.gz` file is never decompressed? Gemini has one good question (Slack/Jira not wired); the other two are close to generic or already answered. |
| 6. Evidence | Both accurate. Claude: 8/8 supported. Gemini: 6 supported and 3 partially. The Postgres node cites the root `init.sql`, which has no `vector(384)` column, instead of `pulse_server/init.sql`. The Slack/Jira question gives the wrong reason for a right conclusion. Its placement note says "client callers are verified in code", but the client and models are hand-written. |

**Correction (A/B 2).** The reader was wrong about `init.sql`: the root file does have `embedding vector(384)`, on line 45. Gemini-medium's Postgres citation is supported, which A/B 2's reader confirmed. The decision does not change.

The reader's overall verdict: a judge would be **meaningfully worse served** by Gemini-first. What it says is accurate, but it leaves out:

- that the demo cannot be traced to the repository;
- that conflict detection cannot match any data the importers write;
- that GitHub data never becomes graph nodes.

So its "demonstrated" dashboard and "AI assistant" framing leave a judge believing the demo shows the repository running. The reader called the Claude review's weaknesses cosmetic: dense references, and a summary that skips the conflict alert.

## Which stage caused the loss

- **observe: not the cause.** The frame-by-frame outputs agree on every frame. Gemini copied slightly less on-screen text: at t=16 one collaboration pair of three, and at t=46 it missed the debt-alert sentence. No review finding depended on the missing text. Gemini's observations did record the "This page says" browser alerts, which is the evidence behind Claude's demo-provenance question.
- **verify: the same in both arms** (Opus 5.5). It did its job: it rejected 10 of Gemini's claims in draft 2, and nothing unsupported reached the final review.
- **generate: the cause.** Gemini's drafts stayed at the level of single files. They never made the cross-file connections behind Claude's key findings: type strings across three services, an unscheduled future call, and demo text missing from the repository. Its repairs answered the verifier by narrowing claims, which left a smaller, safer and less informative review. That is the intended failure mode of the grounding design: a weaker generator produces a thinner review, not a false one.

## Decision

Against the decision rule:

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| No important factual or evidence failure that Claude avoids | Fails: misleading omissions |
| Evidence strength roughly equivalent | Fails: 8 against 11 Demonstrated or Found in code, and weaker targets |
| Clarity stays useful to a judge | Partly |
| Questions stay specific | Fails |
| Cost materially lower | Passes: 4.7× |

**Keep `claude-baseline`** for the pipeline. Gemini-first is not adopted.

**One follow-up test**, if any: Gemini 3.8 Flash at **high** thinking for `generate` only, run on Pulse. Everything else stays fixed:

- `observe` reuses the cached Gemini output. That stage is not in question, and reuse makes the arm cost nothing there.
- `verify` stays on Opus.
- The same blind protocol runs against the same Claude review.

Pass condition: the review surfaces at least two of the three cross-file findings above, with no evidence failures. Expected cost is about $0.6–1.0. If it fails, stop evaluating Gemini for `generate` and look for savings inside the Claude arm instead: the two repairs cost $1.44 of the $2.63.
